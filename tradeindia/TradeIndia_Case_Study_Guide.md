# **About This Case Study**
## **Purpose & Context**
This case study is designed to demonstrate product management thinking from someone transitioning from marketing to PM. It is:

- **A learning exercise:** Not based on internal company data or actual PM experience
- **Data-driven:** Based on analysis of 580 real user reviews from TradeIndia (2025-2026)
- **Framework-focused:** Demonstrates application of PM frameworks (RICE, JTBD, problem trees, metrics hierarchy)
- **Hybrid approach:** Shows how marketing insights inform product decisions and vice versa

## **Who Should Read This**
**Hiring Managers & Recruiters:**

To evaluate product thinking, analytical skills, and ability to translate marketing experience into product strategy.

**Product Managers:**

To see how someone from a different function (marketing) approaches product challenges. May offer fresh perspectives on go-to-market integration.

**Career Transitioners:**

As an example of how to structure a PM case study when you don't have direct PM work experience.
## **Key Limitations to Acknowledge**
This case study is a thought exercise based on publicly available data. It does NOT include:

- Access to internal metrics (actual conversion rates, churn data, revenue figures)
- User interviews or primary research
- Engineering feasibility assessment
- Stakeholder input or cross-functional collaboration
- Competitive analysis or market research beyond user reviews

In a real PM role, these would be critical inputs. This case study demonstrates the thinking process and frameworks, not the full scope of PM work.

# **PM Frameworks Used**
This section provides quick definitions of frameworks applied in the case study:
## **Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)**
**What it is:** A framework for understanding customer motivations by asking 'What job is the user hiring this product to do?'

**Where it's used:** Part 2.2 - Analyzing what sellers/buyers are trying to accomplish beyond surface-level features

**Why it matters:** Helps distinguish between what users say they want (more leads) and what they actually need (ROI-positive business connections)
## **Problem Tree Analysis**
**What it is:** A visual/logical method for mapping symptoms to root causes

**Where it's used:** Part 2.1 - Identifying that lead quality stems from both marketing and product root causes

**Why it matters:** Prevents solving symptoms instead of causes. Reveals that 'low conversion' isn't one problem—it's a symptom of 6 distinct root causes
## **RICE Prioritization**
**What it is:** Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort. A scoring system for prioritizing features/initiatives

**Where it's used:** Part 4.1 - Scoring solutions to determine implementation order

**Why it matters:** Forces data-driven prioritization instead of gut feel. Makes trade-offs explicit (e.g., matching algorithm has high impact but also high effort)
## **Metrics Hierarchy (North Star → Primary → Secondary → Input)**
**What it is:** Structuring metrics from business goals down to feature-level KPIs

**Where it's used:** Part 5.1 - Defining North Star (Lead Conversion Rate) and cascading down

**Why it matters:** Ensures every metric ties to a business outcome. Prevents vanity metrics (e.g., 'feature adoption' without impact on retention)
## **Kano Model (Implicit)**
**What it is:** Categorizes features as basic (expected), performance (more is better), or delighters (unexpected value)

**Where it's used:** Implicitly in Part 3 when distinguishing between 'table stakes' features (verification, basic filtering) vs. delighters (ML-powered matching)

**Why it matters:** Helps prioritize—build basics first, then differentiate. Also informs positioning (marketing can't sell delighters if basics are broken)


# **How This Case Study Demonstrates Marketing → PM Translation**
One unique aspect of this case study is showing how marketing expertise informs product decisions. Here's how:
## **Marketing Insight → Product Requirement Examples**

|**Marketing Insight**|**Product Requirement**|
| :-: | :-: |
|Sales team overpromises to close deals|Build dashboard showing realistic benchmarks by category|
|Verification campaign needs credibility signal|Design verification badge UI and prominence in listings|
|Users don't know how to use new features|In-app onboarding flow and contextual tooltips|
|Acquiring wrong customer segments|Qualification quiz during signup to filter bad fits|

## **Why This Perspective is Valuable**
Many product managers come from engineering or design backgrounds. A marketing background offers:

- **Deep customer acquisition knowledge:** Understanding what brings users to the product (and whether they're the right users)
- **Messaging & positioning expertise:** Knowing how to communicate product value, which features to highlight, and how to manage expectations
- **Funnel optimization mindset:** Seeing the product as part of a larger customer journey, not just isolated features
- **Go-to-market integration:** Natural ability to coordinate product launches with marketing campaigns


# **What's Missing from This Case Study**
To set realistic expectations, here's what a real PM would do differently:
## **User Research**
- **Missing:** User interviews, surveys beyond reviews, observational studies
- **Why it matters:** Reviews capture complaints, not complete user journeys. Interviews would reveal workarounds, context, and unstated needs
- **Next step:** Conduct 15-20 interviews with high/low converting merchants before finalizing solutions

## **Quantitative Baseline**
- **Missing:** Actual conversion rates, churn data, cohort analysis
- **Why it matters:** Can't set realistic targets without knowing current state. '1% conversion' is user-reported, not measured
- **Next step:** Request analytics access to establish baseline before implementation

## **Engineering Feasibility**
- **Missing:** Technical architecture review, data availability assessment, resource constraints
- **Why it matters:** Matching algorithm might require data not currently tracked. Verification APIs might not exist
- **Next step:** Technical discovery with engineering team to scope effort and identify blockers

## **Competitive Analysis**
- **Missing:** How IndiaMART solves this problem, what features differentiate them, pricing comparison
- **Why it matters:** Reviews mention IndiaMART as superior. Understanding why would inform our approach
- **Next step:** Competitive teardown and win/loss interviews with churned users

## **Stakeholder Alignment**
- **Missing:** Buy-in from sales leadership, engineering capacity planning, executive approval
- **Why it matters:** Sales compensation changes require VP approval. Roadmap prioritization competes with other initiatives
- **Next step:** Present this analysis to leadership and secure alignment before proceeding


# **Questions to Ask Me About This Case Study**
If you're interviewing me and want to probe deeper, here are productive questions:
## **Testing Analytical Depth**
- "Walk me through how you calculated the RICE scores. What assumptions did you make?"
- "If your hypothesis about the matching algorithm is wrong, how would you know? What would you measure?"
- "You prioritized sales reform over product features. What made you confident in that decision?"

## **Testing Product Judgment**
- "What if users resist verification because it adds friction? How would you balance trust vs. ease of signup?"
- "Should matching be rules-based or ML-based first? Defend your choice."
- "If you could only build ONE feature from your roadmap, which would it be and why?"

## **Testing Cross-Functional Collaboration**
- "The sales team pushes back on quality metrics. How do you handle it?"
- "Engineering says the matching algorithm will take 9 months, not 3. What do you do?"
- "Marketing wants to launch a big campaign before verification is ready. How do you negotiate?"

## **Testing Learning & Adaptability**
- "You mentioned you have no PM work experience. How would you fill gaps in your knowledge in the first 90 days?"
- "What would you do differently if you had internal data access?"
- "What did you learn from creating this case study that surprised you?"


# **Using This Case Study in Interviews**
## **How to Position This Work**
When discussing this case study in interviews, frame it as:

- **A demonstration of PM thinking:** "I created this case study to show how I approach product problems using frameworks and data, even though I don't have direct PM experience."
- **Evidence of self-directed learning:** "I spent time learning PM frameworks (RICE, JTBD, metrics trees) and applied them to a real-world scenario to test my understanding."
- **A bridge from marketing to product:** "This case study shows how my marketing background helps me see problems holistically—understanding that product issues often start in GTM, and vice versa."

## **What NOT to Say**
- ❌ "This is how TradeIndia should fix their product" (too prescriptive without internal context)
- ❌ "I analyzed user reviews and found the solution" (downplays complexity of PM work)
- ❌ "I have PM experience from this project" (this is a learning exercise, not work experience)

## **What TO Say**
- ✅ "I used this exercise to learn how PMs think about root cause analysis and prioritization"
- ✅ "I recognize this is incomplete without user interviews and internal data—here's what I'd do differently in a real PM role"
- ✅ "My marketing background gives me a unique lens on acquisition and GTM, which I think complements traditional PM skills"

# **Final Thoughts**
This case study is not a substitute for PM work experience. It's a demonstration of product thinking—how to analyze problems, apply frameworks, and design solutions with measurable outcomes.

What it shows:

- Ability to learn and apply PM frameworks independently
- Data-driven analysis and structured problem-solving
- Cross-functional thinking (product + marketing integration)
- Metrics-focused, outcome-oriented mindset

What it doesn't show:

- Real stakeholder management and negotiation
- Working within engineering constraints and timelines
- Adapting to ambiguity and incomplete information in real time
